# Writing quality gate

Use the lightweight pass for ordinary replies; read the full gate for substantial drafts, edits, or audits. This is an editorial contract, not a validated test of whether a human wrote a passage.

## Lightweight pass

Answer the actual question. Use specific supported information, coherent reasoning, an appropriate register, and only useful structure. Remove repeated explanations and empty framing. Preserve uncertainty and voice. End when the reader has what they need, subject to the required output format; do not add a canned moral or generic follow-up offer merely to sound helpful.

## Drafting workflow

1. **Brief:** identify reader, purpose, medium, length, register, required facts, and desired outcome. For a tiny task infer conservatively; do not turn every answer into onboarding.
2. **Substance:** establish the actual answer or argument before phrasing. List supporting facts and uncertainty for complex writing. If details are missing, ask, use labeled placeholders in a draft when appropriate, or state the limit; never manufacture specificity.
3. **Shape:** use the sequence readers need. Put a decision or request early in functional writing; preserve suspense in creative writing and genre-required structure in academic/legal work. No universal three-part or contrast formula.
4. **Compose:** choose accurate verbs and nouns; connect claims through real mechanisms and evidence. Keep necessary technical terms consistent. Rhythm follows thought, not a forced short/long sentence alternation.
5. **Review:** inspect the relevant catalog categories. Make selective edits only where there is an identifiable cost to meaning, voice, reader effort, or task completion.
6. **Compare:** check protected content against the source/brief; inspect every deleted qualification and changed verb of certainty. Read the passage as a whole, not only lint findings.
7. **Deliver:** return one output in the requested mode. If uncertainty or a consequential unresolved issue requires a note, keep it outside ready-to-send copy unless the user asked for annotated text.

Plain-language guidance means matching the reader's knowledge, not forcing every document to a school-grade level; lists, tables, and summaries can help when the task warrants them. ([Digital.gov](https://digital.gov/guides/plain-language/principles))

## Preservation ledger

For consequential edits record the following internally before and after:

| Protected dimension | Check |
|---|---|
| Facts | Names, dates, quantities, units, denominators, ranges, currencies, deadlines |
| Attribution | Who did, said, believed, observed, or recommended what |
| Logical force | Negation, exceptions, only/at least/at most, some/all, if/unless |
| Evidence | Observation versus inference; correlation versus causation; preliminary versus established |
| Modality | May/can/should/must, estimated/confirmed, proposed/approved |
| Scope | Population, geography, timeframe, comparison, limitations |
| Voice | Stance, degree of certainty, politeness, humor, dialect, culturally meaningful details |
| Protected spans | Direct quotations, code, formulas, identifiers, citations and destinations |

Meaning preservation alone is insufficient if an edit changes how knowledgeable, confident, or direct the author appears; research on writing assistance found persona distortions even under some meaning-preserving controls. ([Persona-distortion study](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.22503v1))

## Blockers versus preferences

Block completion on invented facts/sources/experiences, changed protected meaning, false attribution, unrequested commitments, broken exact-output requirements, leaked placeholders or internal notes, or an omitted substantive limitation. Flag consequential unsupported claims rather than smoothing them into certainty.

Treat predictable openings, decorative emphasis, abstract nouns, repetitive transitions, overlong sentences, or repeated rhetorical structures as contextual review candidates. A single word, semicolon, em dash, three-item list, passive construction, or formal tone is not a failure by itself. Genre explains substantial linguistic variation, and aggregate differences do not justify individual authorship verdicts. ([Genre study](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.14111v1), [Wikipedia's guidance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing))

Do not remove “does not establish,” “associated with,” or source limitations when those words express the correct evidentiary boundary. Replace only redundant metacommentary. Never optimize for a detector score by adding mistakes, unusual synonyms, personal claims, or adversarial formatting.

## Acceptance questions

- Does the reader get an answer, decision, explanation, or usable next action rather than a performance of helpfulness?
- Could the generic parts be pasted onto an unrelated topic unchanged? If so, add supported substance or cut them.
- Is each claim supported at the strength expressed? Do examples illustrate rather than smuggle in facts?
- Does the writer still sound like the same person at the requested register?
- Is a paragraph's main idea developed, with no automatic heading for every sentence or closing recap of everything already said?
- Do lists, tables, and emphasis improve comprehension rather than decorate?
- Are quotations/code/citations intact and consequential changes checked?
- Is the output exactly what was requested, without repeated drafts or an unrequested audit?

A checklist pass is an internal review, not evidence that readers preferred the text. Mark user review, blinded comparison, external fact verification, and model-routing tests not run unless they actually occurred.
