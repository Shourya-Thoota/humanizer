# Writing evidence and limits

Research checked October 25, 2026. This is a selected, broad evidence base, not every page on the web. Use it when evaluating a disputed rule, updating patterns, or explaining why a recommendation is contextual. The editorial catalog translates evidence into review questions; its individual checks are not independently validated detector features.

## Empirical studies

| Evidence | Finding relevant to editing | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| [Kobak et al., excess vocabulary](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.07016) | Across 15.1 million PubMed abstracts, abrupt increases in certain style words support corpus-level estimates of LLM-assisted writing. | Does not identify authorship of an individual abstract; vocabulary changes with domain and era. Do not ban the words. |
| [Reinhart et al., PNAS](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2422455122) | Parallel genre corpora show model-dependent differences in nominalizations, participial clauses, coordination, and other linguistic features. | GPT-4o used fewer agentless passives than the human comparison; “AI always uses passive voice” is not supported. Models and genres tested do not represent every system. |
| [Genre analysis, 2026 preprint](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.14111v1) | Study of 11 models, eight genres, and four decoding strategies finds genre and model matter for linguistic profiles. | Do not transfer one genre's optimum style to another or treat these profiles as a universal editing recipe. |
| [Persona distortion, 2026 preprint](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.22503v1) | In UK political-opinion experiments, AI-assisted expression changed reader perceptions of writer traits even under some meaning-preservation controls. | Task-, culture-, and model-specific; preserve stance and degree of confidence, do not infer demographic traits from prose. |
| [Cultural homogenization study](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.11360v3) | In 118 Indian/US participants' writing, AI suggestions increased similarity and shifted some culturally specific content toward Western defaults. | Two national samples are not all cultures; no demographic stereotypes or assumption that every writer from a country shares a style. |
| [Originality incentives, 2026 preprint](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.03529) | In a 200-person short-story experiment, selective revision and originality incentives affected diversity and verbatim adoption. | Semantic diversity is not quality; one task and model cannot prove an editing technique produces human-equivalent writing. |
| [Citation fabrication study](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-023-41032-5) | Historical GPT-3.5/GPT-4 outputs contained fabricated references and errors in real references. | April 2023 results are not current model error rates. Verify existence, metadata, and claim support separately. |
| [English detector-bias pilot](https://arxiv.org/html/2304.02819) | Seven detectors tested in March 2023 falsely flagged many nonnative English essays in a small comparison sample. | Specific detectors and corpora, not an evergreen rate for all languages or tools. |
| [Czech detector-bias counterexample](https://aclanthology.org/2026.eacl-srw.20/) | This study found no systematic nonnative-writer bias across three detector families in its Czech data. | Counterevidence to a universal bias claim, not proof that detectors are universally fair or accurate. |

## Field observations and institutional reporting

| Evidence | Useful observation | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| [Wikipedia: Signs of AI writing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing) | Maintained examples of significance inflation, vague attribution, template rhetoric, markup artifacts, and era-specific vocabulary. | Community field guide, Wikipedia context, not a validated classifier or blanket prohibition on Markdown/formality. |
| [Pew internet analysis](https://www.pewresearch.org/data-labs/2026/08/20/how-much-of-the-internet-is-written-with-ai/) | Aggregate web analysis relates detector estimates and changing punctuation, vocabulary, and rhetorical patterns. | Detector estimates are not verified provenance; Oxford commas, dashes, and particular words do not identify individual authors. |
| [USC study summary](https://today.usc.edu/remembering-the-human-encouraging-unique-voices-in-the-age-of-ai/) | Institutional reporting on a large writing study describes narrower stylistic variation and altered identity cues. | Secondary summary, not a substitute for inspecting the primary methods; do not simplify this to “AI always writes simpler prose.” |
| [University of Zurich editorial guidance](https://www.uzh.ch/blog/ub/2026/09/10/schreibe-ich-schon-wie-eine-ki/?lang=en) | Describes recurring rhetorical templates, unsupported polish, and targeted revision instead of wholesale delegation. | Editorial synthesis and advice, not proof of authorship from any isolated feature. |
| [University of Western Australia commentary](https://www.uwa.edu.au/news/article/2026/august/can-you-teach-yourself-to-detect-ai-writing) | Notes changing tells and task-length mismatch; a short functional message should not acquire an ornate emotional preamble. | Anecdotal claims about “genuinely” are not established markers; Google search trends are not word-use frequencies. |

## Reader-centered style guidance

| Evidence | Practical use | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| [Digital.gov principles](https://digital.gov/guides/plain-language/principles) | Match the audience, organize for understanding, test comprehension. | Not a universal eighth-grade target or a requirement to simplify expert concepts. |
| [Digital.gov short and simple](https://digital.gov/guides/plain-language/principles/short-simple) | Cut unnecessary modifiers and redundant doublets; use consistent terminology. | Government plain-language context; legal/technical terms need substantive review before replacement. |
| [Digital.gov design](https://digital.gov/guides/plain-language/design) | Use meaningful headings, lists, tables, and restrained emphasis to aid scanning. | Formatting itself is not evidence of AI writing; removing useful structure can hurt accessibility. |
| [Microsoft voice guidance](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/style-guide/brand-voice-above-all-simple-human) | Distinguish stable voice from context-sensitive tone; foreground reader needs and actions. | Microsoft brand guidance, not an instruction to make all users sound like Microsoft. |

## Evidence policy and maintenance

Keep four kinds of information distinct: empirical result, institutional summary, community observation, and editorial recommendation. For a new rule record its genre/model/language/timeframe, a concrete reader cost, a counterexample, and a bounded remedy. Prefer a review question over a ban.

Do not invent universality or claim the research proves a passage is human-written. Do not use detector scores as acceptance targets. On explicit research refresh, check changed model-era patterns and opposing findings; do not automatically browse on every writing request. Preserve old examples for context but retire their unconditional rules when evidence or the user's voice conflicts.
