## Read this first: what the signs can and can't tell you

- These are contextual editing observations, not an authorship test. Neither one sign nor a cluster proves who wrote a passage. The underlying field guide itself warns against treating isolated features as proof. ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing))
- A useful failure example is replacing a specific contribution with generic praise: "Inventor of the first train-coupling device" becomes "a revolutionary titan of industry." Restore the supported fact and remove unsupported exaggeration; this illustration is not a universal mechanistic account of language-model behavior.
- Do not use detector outputs or intuition to declare authorship. Detector findings vary by language, corpus, and era; the English pilot and Czech counterexample do not support a universal accuracy or bias claim. In detect mode name concrete writing problems and quote passages. ([English study](https://arxiv.org/html/2304.02819), [Czech study](https://aclanthology.org/2026.eacl-srw.20/))
- These don't indicate AI, so don't flag or "fix" them: perfect grammar; a mix of casual and formal registers; plain or "bland" prose in general; formal or academic prose in general (only specific words are tells); a transition like "Additionally" on its own; missing citations; correct formatting.
- Don't over-scrub. Some constructions are more common in human writing than in AI writing (see "Signs of human writing" near the end). Removing all of them makes text read more like AI.
- Patterns vary by model, genre, and era. Read `writing-evidence.md` for bounded empirical findings; do not use old vocabulary or punctuation lists as a cleanliness test. ([Genre study](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.14111v1))

## Reference

This skill is based on [Wikipedia:Signs of AI writing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing), maintained by WikiProject AI Cleanup (version read October 17, 2026). The patterns documented there come from real examples in Wikipedia articles, drafts, comments, and edit summaries. Wikipedia-only signs (wikitext errors, categories, templates, Articles for Creation quirks) are left out because they don't apply outside Wikipedia. Example passages quoted from Wikipedia are available under CC BY-SA 4.0.

Key insight from Wikipedia: "LLMs ... use statistical algorithms to guess (infer) what should come next based on a large corpus of training material. It thus tends to regress to the mean; that is, the result tends toward the most statistically likely result that applies to the widest variety of cases."
