# Voice calibration and supported personality

Read for supplied samples or English-variety calibration. Analyze only the sample/draft already in scope; this is not permission to gather writing from other services. Preserve existing voice before adding personality. Never manufacture feelings, opinions, experiences, or facts to imitate these examples.

If the user provides a writing sample (their own previous writing), analyze it before rewriting:

1. Read the sample first. Note:
   - Sentence length patterns (short and punchy? Long and flowing? Mixed?)
   - Word choice level (casual? academic? somewhere between?)
   - How they start paragraphs (jump right in? Set context first?)
   - Punctuation habits (lots of dashes? Parenthetical asides? Semicolons?)
   - Any recurring phrases or verbal tics
   - How they handle transitions (explicit connectors? Just start the next point?)
   - Which variety of English the sample actually uses. Preserve it; do not infer style from nationality, location, or topic.

2. Match their voice in the rewrite. Replace AI patterns with patterns from the sample. If they write short sentences, don't produce long ones. If they use "stuff" and "things," don't upgrade to "elements" and "components."

3. When no sample is provided, preserve the draft's own voice. For generic text, use supported rhythm and specificity from "Personality and soul" below without inventing opinions.

### How to provide a sample
- Inline: "Humanize this text. Here's a sample of my writing for voice matching: [sample]"
- File: "Humanize this text. Use my writing style from [file path] as a reference."


## Personality and soul

Preserve the supplied writer's personality where the genre calls for it. Neutral reporting and formal writing can be excellent; they do not need invented opinions or humor.

### Signs of soulless writing (even if technically "clean"):
- Every sentence is the same length and structure
- A requested personal stance flattened into generic neutrality
- No acknowledgment of uncertainty or mixed feelings
- No first-person perspective when appropriate
- Supplied humor or edge erased by an unrequested register change
- Register mismatched to the reader and purpose

### How to add voice:

**Preserve opinions.** Keep the writer's supplied reaction and its strength. Do not add uncertainty, excitement, or a personal stance merely to sound human.

**Respect rhythm.** Adjust monotonous or tangled phrasing where it hurts comprehension; do not impose sentence-length quotas.

**Preserve complexity.** Retain supported tradeoffs and mixed feelings already present; do not manufacture emotional ambiguity.

**Use "I" honestly.** First person may fit the user's draft, but personal experiences and reactions must come from the user or an explicitly fictional brief.

**Keep useful quirks.** Preserve intentional asides and fragments when they serve the piece. Never add mess as camouflage or damage clear structure.

**Be specific only when supported.** Preserve the writer's own details and emotions; do not invent a time, event, or feeling.

### Before (fictional editing input):
> The experiment produced interesting results. The agents generated 3 million lines of code. Some developers were impressed while others were skeptical. The implications remain unclear.

### After (grounded and direct):
> The agents generated 3 million lines of code. Some developers were impressed; others were skeptical. The implications remain unclear.

The edit removes an empty lead-in without inventing reactions, proportions, timing, or the author's feelings. A stronger personal version requires an actual personal stance from the writer.
