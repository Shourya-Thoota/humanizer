# Register-sensitive writing

Read the relevant row before a substantial genre-specific draft or edit. Preserve the user's actual variety and preferences; do not infer a voice from demographic identity or turn one brand's friendly tone into a universal standard.

| Genre | Optimize for | Do not force |
|---|---|---|
| Direct answer | Answer first, needed reasoning, calibrated limits | A title/outline for a tiny answer or a canned conclusion |
| Email/message | Relationship, purpose, specific request/deadline, appropriate courtesy | Artificial intimacy, invented familiarity, universal brusqueness |
| Research report | Evidence, scope, mechanisms, uncertainty, citations | Sales copy, fake certainty, or deletion of methodological limits |
| Academic/technical | Precise terms, definitions, stable notation, reproducibility | Synonym variety, slang, missing qualifiers, lower reading level as a quota |
| Legal/medical/safety | Exact obligations, conditions, risks, uncertainty | Casual rewrites that alter force or remove necessary warnings |
| Marketing | Specific audience benefit and verified proof | Hype in place of facts, fake testimonials or urgency |
| UX/microcopy | Actual state, clear action, recovery, concise labels | Blame, vague errors, cute language during distress |
| Creative/personal | Character, point of view, intended rhythm and imagery | Invented lived experience presented as fact or uniform plainness |
| Multilingual/dialectal | Contextual politeness, local meaning, consistent variety | Automatic Americanization, stereotypes, flattening cultural details |

Voice and tone differ: tone should fit context and the reader's state of mind, even within a consistent brand voice. Microsoft's guidance illustrates this distinction but does not prescribe every writer's personality. ([Microsoft](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/style-guide/brand-voice-above-all-simple-human))

## Grounded examples

All examples below are fictional editing inputs; the “after” preserves only facts supplied by its input.

| Before / request | Appropriate output | Why |
|---|---|---|
| “In order to complete setup, you need to verify your email.” | “To finish setup, verify your email.” | Removes overhead without adding facts |
| “The report is due Friday. Please send your section by Thursday.” | Leave unchanged | Already specific and useful |
| “The pilot may reduce errors, but the sample is small.” | Leave unchanged | Preserves uncertainty and limitation |
| “The system is not approved for clinical use.” | Leave unchanged | Negation is substantive |
| “The study found an association, not causation.” | Leave unchanged | A necessary contrast is not empty rhetoric |
| “Our tool unlocks unparalleled efficiency. It batches up to 20 files.” | “Our tool batches up to 20 files.” | Uses available substance without inventing a measured speedup |
| “Sorry for the inconvenience. Upload failed because the file exceeds 10 MB.” | “The file exceeds 10 MB. Upload a smaller file.” | States cause and supported recovery; courtesy may remain if context calls for it |
| “Please retain the exact quotation: ‘It may help.’” | Retain “It may help.” exactly | Do not turn a quote into “It helps” |
| “Revenue rose from $4m to $5m; margins fell.” | Preserve both changes | Do not rewrite as unqualified business success |
| “I’m annoyed. We agreed Tuesday, and now it’s Friday.” | Preserve the stance and dates | Do not sanitize into corporate appreciation |
| “I wonder if you might be able to review this when convenient.” | Keep or tighten only for the user's relationship/register | Indirectness can be deliberate courtesy |
| “Detect patterns, don't rewrite: This marks a pivotal moment.” | “marks a pivotal moment”: unsupported significance; specify the event's consequence if evidence is available. | Audit mode only, no authorship verdict |

## Counterexamples and new clichés

Do not replace polished boilerplate with manufactured “human” boilerplate: random “honestly,” “look,” swearing, confessions, fragments, rhetorical questions, typos, and fake disagreements can become another formula. Use contractions when they fit, not to meet a quota; keep passive voice when the actor is unknown, irrelevant, or intentionally de-emphasized.

Do not write “I remember,” “I tried,” or “I felt” as an assistant's lived experience. In a user-authored first-person draft, use only experience supplied or approved by that user. Fictional scenes remain possible when clearly requested as fiction.

Preserve meaningful cultural detail and avoid turning a person's own practice into an outsider's generic explanation. A controlled writing study found homogenization toward Western styles in its Indian/US sample; this supports careful preservation, not assumptions about every Indian or American writer. ([Agarwal and colleagues](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.11360v3))
