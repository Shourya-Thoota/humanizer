## Historical signs (older models)

These are historical observations from [Wikipedia's guide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Signs_of_AI_writing), not universal current-model claims. Review the actual writing problem; preserve necessary uncertainty, refusals, and safety boundaries.

### 42. Knowledge-cutoff disclaimers (2022 to 2023)

**Words to watch:** as of my last knowledge update, up to my last training update

**Before:**
> As of my last knowledge update in January 2022, I don't have specific information about the current status of the center.

**After:** Delete it. If the date matters, state the fact with its date.


### 43. Didactic disclaimers (late 2022 to 2024)

**Words to watch:** it's important/critical/crucial to note/remember/consider, worth noting, may vary

**Before:**
> However, it's important to note that these caucuses operate outside the formal ANC structure and their influence on policy decisions may vary.

**After:**
> These caucuses operate outside the formal ANC structure, and their influence on policy decisions may vary.


### 44. Section summaries

**Words to watch:** In summary, In conclusion, Overall

**Problem:** Older models added a "Conclusion" section to long pieces and ended paragraphs by restating their main idea. Cut the restatement and end on the last concrete point.


### 45. Prompt refusals

**Words to watch:** as an AI language model, as a large language model, I cannot offer medical advice, but I can..., I'm sorry...

**Fix:** Remove only pasted boilerplate that does not belong in the deliverable. Do not remove a required refusal, safety boundary, or substantive limitation.


### 46. Abrupt cut-offs

**Problem:** Older chatbots stopped mid-sentence when they hit a length limit. Flag it and leave the ending to the writer, since a bad paste can cause the same thing.


## Signs of human writing (keep these)

These constructions can be useful and should not be scrubbed merely because a pattern list mentions them. Their presence does not establish human authorship, and they should not be inserted to disguise origin:

- Simple is/has phrasing: "there is a," "it has a."
- Plain verbs over stiff synonyms: wrote (not authored), moved (not relocated), used (not utilized), tried (not attempted), died (not passed away).
- Superlative or definitive statements, when true and supported: "one of the best," "the only," "was the first."
- Single qualifiers and intensifiers: very, perhaps, tends to.
- An occasional wordy phrase: as a result of, in order to, all of the, a part of, the fact that.

Model and genre variation matter; consult `writing-evidence.md` rather than relying on permanent stereotypes about particular assistants.
